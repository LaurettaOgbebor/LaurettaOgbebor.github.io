I'm now applying that through a data analytics internship at DecodeLabs. What follows are five projects from that path.

01 EXCEL

Building my first sales dashboard from scratch

Peter led all six sales reps with over 434 million in revenue, Laptop A13 outperformed every other product at over 105 million in profit, and monthly customer counts held steady between 500 and 536 before a sharp drop that's worth flagging to whoever owns that number.

This was the very first data project I built end to end: a regional sales dataset covering products, sales reps, cities, and customer types, with nothing pre summarized for me.

I added calculated columns directly inside an Excel table for Revenue (Unit Price times Quantity), COGS (Cost Price times Quantity), and Profit (Revenue minus COGS), with Cost Price worked out as eighty percent of Unit Price across the dataset. From there I built pivot tables using aggregate functions like SUM and COUNTA to summarize revenue by sales rep, profit by product, COGS by city, and customer counts by month.

Each pivot table got its own chart, bar charts for revenue and profit, a donut chart for COGS by city, a line chart for monthly customers, and everything came together into a single dashboard with KPI cards and slicers for region and product category.

02 EXCEL AT DECODELABS

Cleaning and analysing an ecommerce sales dataset

Resolved 309 missing coupon records without deleting a single row, confirmed all 1,200 orders were unique, and surfaced a revenue decline from 552K in 2023 to 231K in 2025 that any stakeholder would want flagged before the next planning cycle.

This ecommerce dataset came in with 1,200 rows and the kind of mess you actually find in the wild: missing coupon codes, inconsistent date formats, prices with currency symbols baked into the cell.

I filled the blank coupon entries with "No Coupon" instead of deleting anything, reformatted every date to ISO standard, and checked all order IDs for duplicates. From there I built a dashboard tracking total and average revenue, customer count, order status, and revenue by product, with slicers for referral source and coupon use.

Most of this happened on Excel Web after my laptop wouldn't cooperate with the desktop install, which meant no downloadable workbook and some dashboard values had to be pasted in as static figures instead of live formulas. Not the setup I planned for, but it taught me to work around constraints instead of waiting for the ideal one.

03 SQL FUNDAMENTALS

Practicing the fundamentals on a student records table

Went from a blank database to a fully working student records table, with a self incrementing primary key, a working email column, one record removed cleanly, and a check constraint in place so no invalid age can be entered again.

A separate, smaller project built to lock in the basics: creating a table from scratch, inserting records, adding a primary key that increases automatically, renaming a column, updating individual rows, deleting a record, and adding a check constraint so no one under 18 could be added.

It's a different dataset and a different exercise from the DecodeLabs work below, more of a foundations drill than a business analysis, but it's where a lot of the SQL habits used in that project were first practiced.

04 SQL AT DECODELABS

Querying a live business database at DecodeLabs

Flagged every product crossing the 100k revenue mark, identified customers ordering exactly twice for retention follow up, and rebuilt a fully broken price column into usable decimal data.

At DecodeLabs, I write SQL against a 1,200 row order database to answer questions a business would actually ask: which customers use coupons, which orders were paid online, which products cross a 100k revenue mark, which customers have ordered exactly twice.

Along the way I've altered table structures, converted column types, cleaned messy price fields with REPLACE and TRIM, and grouped data to find things like average revenue per order status and total revenue by referral source.

05 POWER BI

Turning raw tables into a working dashboard

Identified Apple as the top profit brand at 197K and white as the leading colour at 39% of sales, then turned both findings into two direct recommendations rather than leaving them as charts on a screen.

Using Power BI, I imported three related tables, Products, Sales and Customers, cleaned them in Power Query using Keep Rows to strip out a block of null data starting at row 250, then connected them through shared keys to build a proper data model instead of working off one flat table.

The result, RealTech Sales Analysis, tracks revenue, cost of goods sold and profit across 250 customers, broken down by brand, colour, region, gender and income level. Medium income customers only lead on total profit because there are more of them, not because each one is worth more, a distinction that matters before anyone builds a strategy on it.

I closed it the way I'd want any report to close: with insights the dashboard doesn't say out loud, and recommendations that follow from them, give Lenovo and Samsung a push since they're close enough to Apple to compete, give Dell and Asus more visibility rather than cutting prices, and stock and market around white and grey since together they drive over 72% of sales.

01. Excel, first sales dashboard+

Objective

Build a full sales analysis end to end in Excel, from raw transactional data to a finished dashboard.

  • Added calculated columns for Revenue (Unit Price times Quantity), COGS (Cost Price times Quantity), and Profit (Revenue minus COGS) directly inside an Excel table.
  • Worked out Cost Price as eighty percent of Unit Price across the dataset.
  • Used pivot tables with SUM and COUNTA to aggregate revenue by sales rep, profit by product, COGS by city, and customer counts by month.
  • Built a chart for each pivot table: bar charts for revenue and profit, a donut chart for COGS by city, a line chart for monthly customers.
  • Assembled everything into a single dashboard with KPI cards and slicers for region and product category.

Tools

Excel (calculated tables, pivot tables, PivotCharts)

Key insights

Peter brought in the highest revenue among sales reps at over 434 million, Laptop A13 was the most profitable product at over 105 million, and customer numbers held steady between about 500 and 536 a month before dropping sharply in the final month of data.

02. Excel at DecodeLabs, ecommerce sales analysis+

Objective

Clean a raw ecommerce order dataset and turn it into a dashboard someone could actually make decisions from.

Process

  • Audited the dataset for missing values, found 309 blank coupon code entries and filled them with "No Coupon" rather than deleting rows.
  • Reformatted every date column to ISO standard so sorting and filtering worked correctly.
  • Verified all 1,200 order IDs were unique and checked the full dataset for duplicate rows, found none.
  • Built pivot tables summarizing revenue by product, order status, and referral source.
  • Assembled a dashboard with KPIs for average revenue, total revenue, total customers, and median revenue, plus slicers for referral source and coupon use.

Tools

Excel (Web version)

Key insights

Chairs generated the highest product revenue at over 195K while phones generated the least. Yearly revenue fell from about 552K in 2023 to 231K in 2025. Cancelled orders were the most common order status.

04. SQL at DecodeLabs+

Answer real business questions from a 1,200 row order database using SQL alone.

Process
